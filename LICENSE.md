## Why two

Neither license alone covers both of the things this project is for.

**PolyForm Noncommercial** permits any noncommercial purpose, and names
research explicitly: *"Personal use for research, experiment, and testing for
the benefit of public knowledge"* is a permitted purpose, as is use by any
*"educational institution, public research organization, public safety or
health organization, environmental protection organization, or government
institution"* — regardless of how that organization is funded. What it does not
clearly cover is a for-profit company using the software to do its own work.

**PolyForm Internal Use** covers exactly that gap: *"Use of the software for the
internal business operations of you and your company is use for a permitted
purpose."* What it does not do is grant the general noncommercial and research
rights above, and it forbids distributing the software at all.

Offered together, they add up to the intent: **research is welcome, internal use
at a company is welcome, and selling it is not.**

## What is not permitted, under either

Neither license lets you sell the software, or sell a product or service that
runs on it. PolyForm Noncommercial rules it out because that is a commercial
purpose. PolyForm Internal Use rules it out because internal business operations
are the only permitted purpose, and it does not allow distribution.

So, concretely:

| | |
|---|---|
| Use it at your job to label your own data | **yes** — Internal Use |
| Academic or government research | **yes** — Noncommercial |
| Modify it for your own purposes | **yes**, under either |
| Publish a paper using results it produced | **yes** |
| Share your changes back | **yes** |
| Sell it, or a fork of it | **no** |
| Run it as a paid service for customers | **no** |
| Bundle it into a product you sell | **no** |

## Irrevocable grant

Neither PolyForm licence says whether the licensor may take it back. This
section says so, and it binds the licensor.

**For every published version of this software, the licensor grants the licences
above perpetually, worldwide, non-exclusively, no-charge, royalty-free and
irrevocably, for the term of the copyright in it.** The licensor will not
withdraw them, will not add conditions to them, and will not assert copyright or
patent rights against anyone acting within them. This binds every present
copyright holder in the project, anyone the copyright is later assigned or sold
to, and the licensor's successors.

The wording is deliberately the wording Apache-2.0 and CC BY 4.0 use for the
same purpose — a stacked grant rather than a promise about one, and scoped to
the term of the copyright rather than to "forever", because copyright does
eventually expire and claiming otherwise would be false.

The one exception is the one already in each PolyForm licence: if you violate
the terms and do not correct it within the notice period that licence gives you,
your licences end automatically. That is a consequence of your own breach, not a
revocation — Apache-2.0 and CC BY 4.0 both pair irrevocability with exactly the
same carve-out.

### What this cannot promise

Being straight about the limit: **the licensor can licence future versions
differently.** Copyright cannot be surrendered that way, and no wording here
would survive it. What is promised is that *what has already been published
stays published on these terms* — so a later version under harsher terms leaves
your copy untouched, and you may fork from the last version you hold.

The practical guarantee is the public record rather than this paragraph. The
commit history and tags of the upstream repository show which versions were
published under these terms, and copies other people hold do not stop existing
because upstream changed its mind.

## Not legal advice

This file is a plain-language summary written by the authors. It has no legal
force. The linked license texts are the actual terms, and where this summary and
those texts disagree, those texts win.

If you are adopting this inside an organization, have your own counsel read the
license you intend to rely on. Both are short, standard, and drafted by the
[PolyForm Project](https://polyformproject.org) — which is much of the point of
using them rather than something bespoke: your legal team can recognise them.
